# Original mastering M1 numerical contract
Version: 0.1 Draft. Date: 2026-09-28.

Approved Slice 002 is active. These are the initial preregistered engineering parameters for the bounded feasibility experiment, not measured quality claims.

- Selected-speech reference pools full 400 ms energies at 100 ms hop from independently reset intervals, then applies the existing absolute/relative gate to the pool.
- Smart uses constant gain if the reference target, programme LRA and -1.5 dBTP internal margin permit it. Otherwise the original Swift planner builds a shared 20 ms gain envelope from measured momentary energy. Night uses the same architecture with stronger levelling and explicit excursion caps.
- Applied linked-envelope bounds: -36 to +12 dB; the reused limiter may add attenuation and never supplies makeup gain. Report these as pre-limiter envelope bounds, not measured total output gain. A request outside these bounds refuses rather than silently changing the target. Dynamic levelling strength starts at `1 - maximumLRA / measuredLRA`, clamped to 0 through 1; Night uses at least 0.75. Two further attempts may increase the strength and correct the measured reference offset. Three candidates maximum.
- Momentary frames align at window centre (200 ms before window end). A 500 ms future minimum anticipates reductions. Gain reduction/recovery smoothing constants: Smart 200/2000 ms, Night 20/500 ms. Interpolate dB linearly between 20 ms points. At levels below max(-55 LUFS, reference -25 LU), hold adaptive gain; no upward adaptation in these windows. Never identify that threshold as speech detection.
- The dynamic path reuses installed FFmpeg's alimiter at four-times source rate, limit -1.5 dBFS, attack 5 ms, release 50 ms, automatic makeup disabled, latency compensation enabled, then returns to the original sample rate. This is a reused peak-control component, not an original limiter or certified true-peak guarantee. Both independent measurement and a separate FFmpeg ebur128 check must accept the actual encoded candidate. The constant-gain path bypasses limiter/resampling.
- No DSP package is added. Existing FFmpeg remains a local, user-installed dependency; dependency distribution is outside this slice. Measure frame count and latency on impulses at all supported rates before accepting this reuse.
- Sources and candidates stream through owned files and bounded buffers. Before/after source hashes and fixed stream/rate/layout contracts bind a plan to its input. Imported reports are never gain authority.

M1 exit requires generated known-answer pooling, constant-gain, linked stereo, peak-limited feasibility, latency/frame-count and bounded-noise tests. Failure is investigated without widening the Slice 002 acceptance tolerances. Listening quality is an M2/M3 gate and is not inferred from M1.

Initial experiment: energy pooling, constant FLAC candidate/publication, a 30-second 20 dB transition Night candidate, and impulse latency/frame count/true peak at all five rates passed in release tests. Additional tests passed for quiet-tail non-increasing gain, phase-opposed stereo balance, silent/unconfirmed speech refusal, changed-source refusal, and constant PCM identity within two 24-bit LSBs. The quiet-tail range conflict is a verified refusal case, not a successful 3 LU master. Original preview staging uses float64 WAV to preserve over-full-scale decoded values; playback must attenuate safely. Scratch estimate includes that larger original.

M1 numerical feasibility exit: eight tests passed in 7.484 seconds (release). Independent integrated loudness/peak use the unpadded candidate; only the independent LRA pass uses 1.5 seconds of padding, matching the Swift meter tail policy. The initial padded integrated comparison was rejected and corrected without changing tolerances. Full regression, EBU, long-file, listening and native UI gates remain open.

Gain-statistics clarification before M2: the reported min/max are the exact applied Swift envelope extrema before the reused limiter. They must be labelled as such in UI and receipts. The limiter can add attenuation, so these numbers are not represented as measured total gain extrema. Its makeup is disabled; final peaks and loudness are measured independently. Characterizing the limiter's time-varying attenuation remains a verification-report limitation, not an invented statistic.

## Registered revision 2 experiment

The licensed full-film Smart trial at -23 LUFS/11 LU produced 16.67 LU LRA after three attempts; Night at -30 LUFS/3 LU produced 11.18 LU LRA. Both withheld output. These results prove failure of this planner under these requests, not mathematical infeasibility of every possible algorithm. Lowering target alone was insufficient.

Before the next implementation experiment: change the adaptive levelling control from centre-aligned 400 ms momentary energy to centre-aligned 3-second short-term energy, the statistic on which LRA is based. Extend the first/last available full short-term window across boundary positions. Retain the 400 ms quiet/noise hold, Night momentary/short-term caps, smoothing, 500 ms future minimum, gain bounds, limiter, three-attempt bound and all acceptance tolerances. This tests whether rapid within-speech variation was being controlled at the expense of the requested programme range. It is a hypothesis, not a proven root cause or quality improvement. Re-run generated gates before the full-film trial and keep earlier failed evidence.

## Registered revision 3 experiment

Revision 2 also failed the licensed film: Smart -23/11 ended at 16.74 LU LRA; Night -30/3 at 10.33 LU. Preserve these failures. The next bounded experiment uses 20 ms K-weighted source energy bins and predicts window energies under the proposed shared gain. These are an internal planning approximation, never output measurement authority. The existing meter and report v1 retain their definitions.

Before implementation: allow at most 24 in-memory feedback iterations, then retain the existing maximum of three actual renders from the original. Predict 400 ms integrated blocks and 3-second range blocks at 100 ms hop, including the existing LRA-only terminal padding. On each iteration, centre a band of width max(0.5, requested LRA - 0.5) on the predicted range's 10th/95th percentile midpoint. Distribute window corrections, each bounded to 1.5 dB, over their participating 20 ms bins and average overlaps. For programme reference, add a predicted integrated correction bounded to 1 dB per iteration. Selected-speech reference continues to use fresh rendered measurement for its outer correction. Keep absolute gain bounds, quiet hold, Night excursion caps, look-ahead and smoothing. Stop after the fixed iteration limit; actual encoded acceptance remains unchanged. Add cancellation checks within each iteration. Test prediction independently against a constant-gain known answer and retain silence/tail boundaries. This is a planner experiment, not speech detection or a change to multichannel scope.

Revision 3 generated evidence: 12 tests in two suites passed in 6.469 seconds, including the new prediction-versus-meter check and all three cancellation phases. A former quiet-tail refusal now produces a verified candidate; that historical refusal was a planner limitation, not a proof of conflicting requirements. The test now checks the unchanged output limits. A separate source requiring more than +12 dB verifies refusal and owned cleanup. Revision 3 planning runs off the main actor with cancellation propagation; its initial plan is reused for the first render. Film, EBU, resource and listening results remain separate gates.

## Registered revision 4 experiment

Revision 3 predicted and measured film results agreed to about 0.01 LU, but failed targets: first Smart -26.15 LUFS/13.32 LU; first Night -34.64 LUFS/5.53 LU. Three attempts ended at -25.94/13.46 and -34.26/5.93 respectively. This implicates the constrained gain plan, not a large prediction error on this source. No candidate was published.

Before implementation: replace causal attack/release smoothing with a centred Gaussian in dB, radius 500 ms, sigma 200 ms Smart and 80 ms Night. Retain the 500 ms future minimum before smoothing. Permit recovery during a below-floor interval only when a measured above-floor interval is already known within the next 500 ms; otherwise retain non-increasing quiet-tail gain. This prepares for known foreground energy, not an inference of speech. Preserve the -36/+12 dB limits, 24 feedback iterations, three actual-render limit, original-sample rendering, and every output tolerance. This is a bounded test of recovery lag; it is not proof of that root cause or listening quality. The local film preparation gate first checks predicted loudness/range and skips costly rendering when the first plan already misses those limits; prediction never authorizes publication. Generated tests continue to exercise the full rendering path.

## Registered revision 5 experiment

Revision 4 still failed the film preflight: Smart predicted -26.32 LUFS; Night -33.84 LUFS with 5.53 LU range. No full film candidate was rendered. Centred smoothing alone did not resolve the failure.

Before implementation: cap the desired gain in below-floor intervals at the base offset, when no above-floor foreground is known in the next 500 ms. This removes retained positive adaptive boost from quiet background after an amplified passage, rather than allowing a previous +12 dB boost to persist through that background. It can only reduce desired gain in those intervals. Apply the existing look-ahead and centred smoothing to this ceiling and retain non-increasing quiet tails. Keep the same threshold, all gain/output limits and iteration count. Hypothesis: retained boost moves formerly quiet background into the output's relative LRA gate. This is not a speech gate, a source separator, or proof of that hypothesis. Compare the preregistered film requests and generated continuity tests before adopting it.

## Registered revision 6 experiment

Revision 5 film preflight still failed: Smart -23.84 LUFS/12.82 LU and Night -33.28 LUFS/5.58 LU. Quiet-background gain control helped the Smart reference but did not establish compliance. No film render was made.

Before implementation: shorten the future-minimum attenuation window from 500 ms to 60 ms (three 20 ms intervals). Keep the centred 3-second levelling control, Gaussian smoothing, foreground recovery horizon, all bounds, limiter, feedback count and acceptance unchanged. Hypothesis: early attenuation of quieter material preceding louder events is unnecessarily reducing short-term window energy. The reused oversampled limiter and actual-output peak checks remain mandatory. This is the final smoothing/look-ahead variant in this bounded experiment; if it still fails, preserve the best documented baseline and reopen the planning objective before further algorithm variants.

## Retained baseline and checkpoint

Revision 6 still missed preflight: Smart predicted -24.17 LUFS/13.14 LU; Night -32.50 LUFS/5.57 LU. The Gaussian, quiet-background ceiling and shorter look-ahead variants did not establish film compliance. Retain revision 3, whose full rendered film was independently measured and whose prediction matched that output. Its failure remains explicit; retaining it is not acceptance. Versions 4 through 6 are recorded experiments, not selectable product modes.

The bounded smoothing experiment is closed. D-015 is reopened. Before further algorithm variants, revise the planning objective with an explicit analysis of gain-cap feasibility and relative-gate changes on sparse soundtracks. Keep the existing acceptance contract until a replacement brief is reviewed. Neither a lower target nor a widened LRA tolerance is silently substituted. The native experimental workflow and output-verification safeguards can be reviewed in a draft PR; the listening pack and production qualification remain blocked by the film gate.
