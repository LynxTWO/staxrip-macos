# Video-copy feasibility and verification design
Version: 0.1. Date: 2026-10-01.

FFmpeg's [stream-copy documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Streamcopy) describes forwarding encoded packets without video decoding/filtering/encoding. Its [ffprobe options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html#Main-options) expose per-packet payload hashes and codec extradata hashes. These primitives support verification; process success or codec names alone do not establish preservation.

Generated FFmpeg 9.0.2 discovery copied a three-second 160 by 96, 24 fps H.264 MP4 into MKV and back. All three contain 72 identical ordered packet payload hashes and identical extradata. Independent raw decoded video hashes and frame counts match. Presentation times differ by at most 0.333 ms as the container time base changes. MKV omits the initial two negative decoder timestamps and quantizes packet durations; MP4 round-trip duration is 2.999 instead of 3.000 seconds. Do not equate container bytes or decoder timestamp representation with picture preservation.

A generated HEVC VFR sequence with alternating frame intervals also retains 72 packet hashes, extradata and decoded pixel hashes through both containers. Presentation times match exactly in this fixture. Packet durations differ by up to one millisecond after mux quantization, including the round trip from a millisecond source time base. The proposed contract checks both PTS and positive duration against the larger supported source/output tick, capped at one millisecond.

A copied AAC track delayed to 0.5 seconds retains 95 packet hashes through MP4 to MKV. Its first packet is 0.478667 versus 0.479 seconds because of priming and container precision; video remains zero-start. An ordinary zero-onset AAC source retains zero video PTS while the first audio packet is negative (about 21 ms), and MKV declares 3.021 seconds rather than 3.000. Copied audio and re-encoded audio must remain distinct choices. Independent numerical alignment checks are required; no owner listening or arbitrary-player claim follows from these packets.

Two negative references demonstrate why the new mode needs a complete audit. A 125 ms offset retains all video payload hashes but shifts every presentation timestamp. Dropping one B-frame packet yields 71 packets while the container still reports three seconds. Both can fit the existing generic 250 ms duration check. The new copy contract must reject them before publication.

The initial supported subset is deliberately explicit: upright square-pixel progressive 8-bit SDR H.264/HEVC in MP4/QuickTime or Matroska. No trim or picture filter can apply to copied video. A validated existing codec/encoder pair avoids optional-field downgrade ambiguity in older readers. Runtime capture and comparison use private bounded staging and strict framing, with source identity and existing exclusive publication retained. Full films, arbitrary edit lists, decoder timestamp equivalence, HDR and other formats remain unqualified.
